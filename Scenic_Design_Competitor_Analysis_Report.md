## Executive Summary

This competitive analysis examines four leading scenic designer portfolio websites to identify design patterns, content strategies, and unique positioning approaches within the theatrical design industry. The analysis compares these competitors against Brandon PT Davis's current website to provide actionable recommendations for enhancing competitive positioning while maintaining the site's distinctive "ART × TECHNOLOGY × DESIGN" identity.

**Key Finding:** Brandon's site demonstrates significantly more content depth and technical sophistication than competitors, with integrated articles, news, and educational resources. ## Competitor Overview

### 1. Alexander Dodge Design

**Website:** alexanderdodgedesign.com  
**Career Level:** Tony-nominated Broadway designer, Metropolitan Opera debut  
**Primary Strength:** Extensive biographical narrative and awards emphasis

Alexander Dodge's portfolio opens with a full-screen carousel showcasing dramatic production photography from major venues including the Metropolitan Opera and Broadway. The site employs a dark background with high-contrast white typography, creating an elegant and theatrical atmosphere. Navigation remains minimal with five primary sections: HOME, ABOUT, PORTFOLIO, NEWS, and CONTACT.

The homepage features an extensive 500-word biography that weaves personal narrative (born in Switzerland, raised at Frank Lloyd Wright's Taliesin West) with professional achievements. Awards and nominations receive prominent placement throughout the text, establishing credibility through recognition from major institutions. The biography mentions specific training ("studied with Ming Cho Lee at Yale School of Drama") and current representation by United Talent Agency, signaling professional stature.

Project organization follows disciplinary categories (Opera, Musical, Theater, Dance) rather than chronological ordering. The news section highlights press mentions and interviews, extending the site's function beyond portfolio display into media presence documentation. Contact information includes both studio and mobile numbers plus agent details, facilitating multiple pathways for professional inquiry.

### 2. Derek McLane

**Website:** derekmclane.com  
**Career Level:** Multiple Tony Award winner, Oscar ceremony designer  
**Primary Strength:** Ultra-minimalist confidence and crossover work emphasis

Derek McLane's approach represents the opposite extreme from content-heavy portfolios. The homepage consists of a single full-screen production photograph (Moulin Rouge on Broadway) with centered white text overlay displaying only his name and five navigation categories. No biography, no awards list, no explanatory text—the work speaks entirely for itself.

This radical minimalism communicates supreme confidence in the designer's reputation and work quality. The navigation structure reveals strategic positioning choices: PLAYS, MUSICALS, OSCARS, ARCHITECTURE & INTERIORS, and ABOUT. The "OSCARS" category as a standalone section emphasizes crossover success beyond theatre, while "ARCHITECTURE & INTERIORS" signals design versatility across disciplines.

The site's visual impact derives from dramatic production photography and bold typography against dark backgrounds. The absence of text on the homepage forces visitors to engage directly with project imagery, creating immediate visual impact without narrative mediation.

### 3. Michael Schweikardt Design

**Website:** msportfolio.com  
**Career Level:** Regional theatre designer with 60+ production credits  
**Primary Strength:** Comprehensive documentation and artistic breadth

Michael Schweikardt's portfolio emphasizes breadth and thoroughness over dramatic presentation. The homepage displays a grid of production photographs representing 60+ designs, immediately communicating extensive experience. Each project listing includes complete creative team credits (director, costume designer, lighting designer), demonstrating collaborative practice and professional generosity.

Navigation extends beyond standard portfolio sections to include "Figure Drawing" and "Writing" categories. These additions position the designer as a complete artist rather than solely a theatrical technician. The figure drawing section showcases foundational artistic skills, while the writing section presents essays and reflections on design practice.

The site employs a clean white background with straightforward typography, prioritizing clarity and accessibility over dramatic visual impact. Projects receive equal visual weight in the grid layout, avoiding hierarchical presentation that might emphasize certain productions over others. This democratic approach suggests a designer comfortable with the full range of their work rather than highlighting only prestigious credits.

### 4. Mia Irwin Design

**Website:** miairwindesign.com  
**Career Level:** Emerging designer, MFA graduate, studio assistant to Derek McLane  
**Primary Strength:** Academic achievement emphasis and skills diversification

Mia Irwin's portfolio demonstrates how emerging designers establish credibility through education, awards, and skill diversification. The homepage features a dramatic full-screen production photograph with a distinctive pink banner overlay displaying her name—creating memorable brand identity through color choice. The tagline "Scenic Designer" appears in large white typography, establishing clear professional identity.

The navigation structure reveals strategic positioning for an emerging career: "Realized Scenic Designs," "Theoretical Scenic Designs," "Scenic Painting," "Studio Art," and "Properties." The inclusion of "Theoretical Scenic Designs" acknowledges academic work and unproduced concepts, expanding the portfolio beyond realized productions. This approach demonstrates design thinking and conceptual skills even when production opportunities remain limited.

Awards receive prominent homepage placement with hyperlinked mentions of the USITT Scene Design Award and Penn State Creative Achievement Award. The biography mentions current employment as Studio Assistant for Derek McLane, leveraging association with an established designer to build credibility. Upcoming projects receive equal billing with completed work, suggesting forward momentum and continued opportunity.

## Competitive Disadvantages: Brandon's Site

### 1. Visual Impact Dilution

Brandon's content-rich homepage with multiple sections (Featured Projects, Latest Thinking, Latest News, Articles) delays immediate engagement with portfolio work. Competitors Derek McLane and Alexander Dodge create instant visual impact through full-screen production imagery. Visitors seeking quick portfolio assessment may feel overwhelmed by content density.

### 2. Navigation Complexity

The six-item main navigation plus dropdown menus for WORK, ABOUT, and STUDIO creates more cognitive load than competitors' simpler structures. New visitors must process more options before locating desired content. Competitors' five-item flat navigation enables faster orientation.

### 3. Homepage Hero Section Size

Brandon's hero section occupies approximately 60% of viewport height, leaving 40% for navigation and tagline. Competitors Alexander Dodge and Derek McLane use 100% viewport height for hero imagery, creating more dramatic first impressions. The scroll indicator suggests content below, but visitors may not scroll if the hero section doesn't compel them.

### 4. Project Photography Presentation

The scenic design portfolio page uses a three-column grid with moderate-sized images. Competitors Michael Schweikardt and Alexander Dodge employ larger imagery that showcases production photography detail more effectively. Theatre design is fundamentally visual; larger images communicate design quality more immediately.

### 5. Awards and Recognition Visibility

Brandon's awards and recognition appear on dedicated resume/about pages rather than homepage or portfolio pages. Competitors Alexander Dodge and Mia Irwin prominently feature awards to establish credibility. For visitors unfamiliar with Brandon's work, the absence of visible recognition may reduce perceived authority.

## Strategic Recommendations

### Priority 1: Enhance Visual Impact on Homepage

**Current State:** The homepage hero section features large typography and tagline but no production imagery. Content sections below include project cards with images, but these appear only after scrolling.

**Recommendation:** Implement a full-screen hero section with rotating production photography (similar to Alexander Dodge's carousel) while maintaining the name, tagline, and CTA buttons as overlay elements. This approach combines Brandon's strong positioning statement with competitors' dramatic visual presentation.

**Implementation:** Create a background image carousel featuring 3-5 of Brandon's most visually striking production photographs. Ensure images are high-resolution and showcase diverse work (comedy, drama, musical, Shakespeare). Maintain current typography and CTA buttons as overlay elements with subtle drop shadows for readability. Add subtle animation (slow zoom or parallax) to create visual interest without distraction.

**Expected Impact:** Increased time on site, reduced bounce rate, stronger first impression for new visitors.

### Priority 2: Simplify Primary Navigation

**Current State:** Six main navigation items (WORK, NEWS, ABOUT, ARTICLES, STUDIO, CONTACT) plus dropdown menus create complexity that may overwhelm visitors.

**Recommendation:** Consolidate navigation to five primary items following industry standard: WORK, ABOUT, INSIGHTS, STUDIO, CONTACT. Merge NEWS and ARTICLES into a single "INSIGHTS" section with internal filtering. This simplification reduces cognitive load while maintaining content access.

**Implementation:** Create a unified Insights landing page with filter tabs for Articles, News, and Tutorials. Maintain dropdown functionality for WORK (Scenic Design, Experiential, Rendering, Models) and STUDIO (Tutorials, App Studio, Vault, Scenic Directory). Update navigation component and test mobile responsiveness.

**Expected Impact:** Faster visitor orientation, reduced decision paralysis, improved mobile navigation experience.

### Priority 3: Increase Portfolio Image Sizes

**Current State:** The scenic design portfolio page displays projects in a three-column grid with moderate-sized images (approximately 400px wide on desktop).

**Recommendation:** Implement a two-column grid on desktop (expanding to single column on mobile) with larger images (approximately 600px wide). This change prioritizes visual impact while maintaining browsable grid structure. Add hover effects that slightly enlarge images and reveal additional project details.

**Implementation:** Modify portfolio grid CSS to use two columns on desktop breakpoints. Implement smooth hover transitions that scale images 1.05x and reveal overlay with director name and additional metadata. Ensure images maintain aspect ratio and load progressively for performance.

**Expected Impact:** Improved design detail visibility, stronger portfolio impact, better showcase of production photography quality.

### Priority 4: Add Awards Section to Homepage

**Current State:** Awards and recognition appear only on resume/about pages, invisible to homepage visitors.

**Recommendation:** Create a concise awards section on the homepage between Featured Projects and Latest Thinking sections. Display 3-5 major recognitions with institution logos and brief descriptions. Link to full resume for complete credentials.

**Implementation:** Design a horizontal awards showcase with subtle animations on scroll. Include recognitions such as academic honors, professional awards, notable theatre credits (South Coast Repertory, regional theatre debuts), and any design competition placements. Use institution logos where available to create visual credibility markers.

**Expected Impact:** Increased credibility for unfamiliar visitors, improved conversion for potential clients, stronger competitive positioning against award-emphasizing competitors.

### Priority 5: Implement Project Detail Page Enhancements

**Current State:** Project detail pages include production photos, renderings, creative team, and design notes. Layout follows consistent template across all projects.

**Recommendation:** Maintain current comprehensive content while enhancing visual presentation. Implement larger hero images, add image lightbox functionality for full-screen viewing, and create more dramatic transitions between content sections.

**Implementation:** Expand hero image to full viewport width. Add click-to-enlarge functionality for all production photos and renderings. Implement smooth scroll animations that reveal content sections progressively. Consider adding video content for select projects (time-lapse builds, production footage, designer commentary).

**Expected Impact:** Improved engagement on project pages, longer session duration, better showcase of design details and process.

### Priority 6: Optimize Mobile Experience

**Current State:** Site is responsive but optimized primarily for desktop viewing. Production photography and grid layouts compress on mobile devices.

**Recommendation:** Conduct comprehensive mobile audit and implement mobile-first design improvements. Prioritize touch-friendly navigation, optimized image sizes for mobile bandwidth, and simplified layouts that maintain visual impact on smaller screens.

**Implementation:** Test site across multiple mobile devices and screen sizes. Implement mobile-specific image optimization (WebP format, responsive srcset). Simplify mobile navigation with hamburger menu that reveals full site structure. Ensure CTA buttons are thumb-friendly (minimum 44px height). Add swipe gestures for image galleries.

**Expected Impact:** Improved mobile user experience, reduced mobile bounce rate, better accessibility for on-the-go portfolio reviews.
